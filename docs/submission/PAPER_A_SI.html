Supplementary Note 2: compute and repeats

The training settings are in the Methods (Systems). On MuLMS's decision seeds a CRF head run takes 96 s on a T4 (the best epoch after 8.1 epochs on average), an MR3 run 198 s (5.7) and an MR6 run 296 s (5.1). A names run's stored time is its job's: the three teachers, their tagging of the corpus and the job's three or four students together, 2,099 s on average over the 14 jobs (1,598–2,972 s; the best epoch after 5.6 epochs). Per student that is 609 s (the job's time over its students, averaged over the 14 jobs), 6.4 times the CRF head's 95.8 s; the harvest alone, the job less its students' own training at MR6's rate, is about 1,063 s, eleven CRF-head runs, and a single student charged with the whole harvest costs about 14 times the CRF head's run. None of these figures counts building the corpus (the search, download and filtering of about 300 articles). The students see 2.8 to 4.6 times more sentences per epoch (601, 1,203 and 1,209 augmented sentences on average beside the 340 gold ones, for MR3, MR6 and the names) and reach their best epoch after 5.7, 5.1 and 5.6 epochs against the CRF head's 8.1, so up to it they take 2.0, 2.9 and 3.1 times its updates. Runs used Kaggle sessions of two T4 GPUs with deterministic CUDA, one frozen commit per batch, a pinned image and pinned libraries; the three week-14 batches alone took 19 session hours (5.2, 4.3 and 9.7). The two week-17 sessions took 4.7 and 3.1 hours. A names job on the multi-task base (three multi-task teachers, their tagging of the corpus with two heads, ten students) took 82.5 to 122.7 minutes (mean 99.7, by the results files' run times; the session manifests give 82.7 to 122.9, mean 99.9), 10.0 minutes per student with the harvest shared over its ten students; the harvest alone, the job less ten students at the control's rate, is about 31 minutes, so a student charged with all of it costs about 38. A multi-task six-copy control run takes 6.9 minutes, a three-copy one 4.8, the multi-task model 2.0, and a CRF-head run 1.1 fine-grained and 1.3 coarse-grained on the same papers; the students' best epoch came after 5.4 to 8.1 epochs on average per paper and granularity.

Every interval in the paper is the analysis scripts' value, computed from per-unit values (F1 means over seeds, or rates pooled over seeds) rounded to two decimals; recomputed from the unrounded runs, no bound moves by more than about 0.01 and no verdict changes.

Repeats. From week 5 on (15 October, 16:00 UTC), every GPU session that completed, except two, began by repeating an earlier run, and every repeat was identical (the sessions that failed or hung left no record of their jobs: five on 17 October, before week 10, whose bundle had a new version within about 20 minutes of their push, and two of weeks 10 to 18, items 4 and 5 of Supplementary Note 11): one batch of 15 October (w5a, run 16:05 to 22:10 UTC) ran none, and the second session of the follow-up of 31 October (19:15) (w17b) instead reproduced the stored multi-task runs' scores through its teachers. The week-4 sessions of 15 October, which wrote the CRF head's first GPU reference runs, began without a repeat after a smoke session that ran one CRF run three times, with identical scores and predictions.

Supplementary Note 5: what the names do, in detail

Everything in this Note is post hoc, except the companion study's decisions quoted at its end.

Rates. Over all ten seeds the names system makes, net of the predictions it drops, 474 more unseen-name predictions per run than MR6 and 143 more correct ones, a net rate of 30% (MR3's net additions over the CRF head, 43%; MR6's over MR3, 33%); these are differences of pooled counts, not the precision of the predictions one system makes and the other does not. On the decision seeds alone the pooled trade against MR6 is 3.0 points of recall for 2.9 of precision and +0.5 F1 on unseen names (Table 4's note). The mixed corpus's figures are similar (46.7% recall at 52.4 precision, against the topic corpus's 46.9% at 51.3 on the same seeds), and its unseen-name F1 is slightly higher (49.35 against 49.0). On MaterioMiner's papers, fine-grained, the names add to both pooled rates on unseen names (39.7% to 41.1% recall and 49.2 to 53.2 precision over MR6; F1 43.9 to 46.4), and coarse-grained a little of each (53.0% to 54.2% and 52.4 to 53.2; F1 52.7 to 53.7). Paired over the four papers the changes are +0.86 [−3.38, +5.10] in recall and +1.32 [−6.62, +9.25] in precision fine-grained, and +1.60 [−1.37, +4.57] and +1.16 [−2.24, +4.57] coarse-grained, every interval covering 0.

Per subfield, the lexicon's coverage of the unseen test names runs from 7% (semiconductors) to 36% (electrolysis), and the names' gain over MR6 on the decision seeds from −2.3 (polymers) to +3.3 (solid oxide fuel cells); the two correlate negatively (Pearson r = −0.71, Spearman −0.61, 7 points), and the gain also correlates with how weak the CRF head is on the subfield (r = −0.76 with its F1; semiconductors at 32.5 and solid oxide fuel cells at 43.5 gain most). The two readings cannot be separated here. Coverage and the baseline's F1 are themselves correlated (r = 0.89); given F1, coverage keeps almost no association with the gain (partial r = −0.10) while F1 given coverage keeps some (−0.40), but with 7 points, 4 degrees of freedom for a partial correlation, neither is distinguishable from 0, and the raw correlations fall to −0.19 and −0.01 without the two subfields that drive them (semiconductors and solid oxide fuel cells). The raw correlation with coverage runs against a simple dose–response in F1, though the membership split (main text, Results, What the names do) finds the recall gain on the held names in every subfield; F1 also pays the precision the names cost.

The lexicon split in full. Table S3 gives the levels and every paired comparison of the analysis in the main text (Results, What the names do), and Supplementary Note 13 each unit's gains and the unseen-name trade of every system against the one it builds on, in F1, recall and precision. A test name is unseen by the main text's words-only rule (Methods, Data) and in the lexicon when the lexicon holds its words with its type; a prediction is classed the same way by its words and predicted type, and the precision of each part is the share of its unseen predictions that are correct. On MuLMS's decision seeds the names add 8,336 predictions of names the lexicon holds, 39% of them right, and 12,206 of other names, 22% right (419 more unseen-name predictions per run, 29% right), so two thirds of the wrong predictions they add are names the lexicon does not hold, and 45% of the added correct ones too; precision falls on both parts, 74.7% to 69.0% on the held names and 46.4% to 44.0% on the others. The mixed-corpus system's own lexicon holds 18.3% of the unseen test names, not the topic lexicon's 22.2% by which its rows are split.

The reading rule. Fixed before the run (31 October 16:13, 2c60f33): "Name knowledge: the names' recall gain over MR6 is larger on the unseen names the lexicon holds than on those it does not, the paired interval of the difference above 0. A generic shift: the two gains are alike, the interval covering 0 with both gains positive. Mixed: the interval above 0 but the gain on the names not held also above 0." The rule does not say whether a gain is read on its point estimate or its interval. On MuLMS the difference's interval is above 0 (+6.17 [+2.44, +9.90]) and the gain on the names not held is +1.12 [−0.78, +3.02]: mixed on its point estimate, name knowledge on its interval. On MaterioMiner both gains are positive at both granularities and the difference's interval covers 0 (fine-grained +4.33 [−5.33, +13.98], coarse-grained −1.54 [−3.60, +0.51]): a generic shift on point estimates, and none of the three outcomes on intervals, every gain interval covering 0. The outcome note of 31 October read MuLMS as name knowledge and MaterioMiner as a generic shift, the first on intervals and the second on point estimates; read one way throughout, the rule gives mixed and a generic shift, or name knowledge and none.
